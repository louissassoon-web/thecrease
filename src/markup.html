Restart match?

Share game

Anyone with the testing link can play. Each browser has its own matches and saves.

When a shot looks wrong, copy the ball details and send them with what you expected. A screen recording helps with movement or graphics.

Build 35. Your save is not included in the game link or ball details.

Find the middle

Watch the ball. Swipe on the pitch or the pad beneath it, then lift your thumb when the ball reaches the bat. You can hold a swipe before releasing.

Direction
Up is straight. Directions follow the field view. Off and leg sides swap for a left-hander; the guide swaps with them. Downward diagonals play behind square.
Ground & loft
A direct swipe plays along the ground. To loft, pull your thumb back, then reverse towards your target without lifting. Look for LOFT READY, then release when the ball reaches the bat. A diagonal follow-through works too; you do not need to retrace your path. The forward part sets direction and power. Late cuts, leg glances and paddle sweeps stay guided along the ground. A small forward movement without a backswing defends.
Forward defence
Make a small forward movement and release at contact. The batter steps into fuller balls with a straight bat and soft hands. Defensive timing is more forgiving than an attacking stroke.
Back foot
On a short ball, swipe towards the leg side for a pull at waist height or a hook higher up. A straight swipe plays a compact punch below shoulder height. An off-side diagonal selects a cut on short balls. Fuller and lower good-length balls offer drives. Direct swipes stay grounded, including firm hooks and punches.
Leave
Keep your thumb off the pad. A short defensive swipe also ducks a high bouncer. A ball on the stumps can still bowl you.
Read the bounce
Yorkers need the bat down at your toes. Step into full balls to drive. Good lengths rise towards the body. Move back for cuts and pulls on short balls. The ball's shadow helps you judge its height. Against fuller spin, swipe square on the leg side for a sweep or finer behind square for a paddle. Draw back, then swipe towards the leg side for a slog sweep. Short spin offers pulls, cuts and back-foot punches. The guide changes with the bowler. Against pace, use the back foot for cuts and pulls.
Player strengths
Pause and open Player profiles to see both teams. Timing, power, defence, pace and spin control, and preferred shots affect how each batter plays. Bowlers have individual pace, control, movement and variations. Profiles differ by format. Lower ratings are relative weaknesses, so play to your batter’s strengths. Editing names keeps the underlying player.
View & sound
Pause with the menu button and open Settings. Full-screen batting removes the lower pad so you swipe on the field; switch it off to restore the pad. Sound is switched on or off here. Reduced motion removes camera travel and uses static celebration messages. The game stays paused while you change settings. Restart match replays the fixture; Restart entire game clears both careers after confirmation.
Bowling
Choose a bowler before the over, then line, variation and field before each ball. Swipe upwards and lift as the arm comes over. Earlier release is fuller; later is shorter. Switch off the pitch marker in Settings when you no longer need it. The bowling offset is separate from your batting calibration. Simulate over finishes the current over; Simulate innings completes your bowling innings.
Outfield catches
A fielder calls automatically. On an aerial ball into the outfield, tap an available name to change the caller before the attempt. Close reflex catches stay automatic. A difficult catch may be dropped; watch the ball until the return is complete. The aerial trail and six distance help judge the flight.
Timing offset
Settings can shift where a perfect swing is measured, from -200 to +200 ms. Use a negative offset if you naturally release early, or positive if late. After 20 balls, or at the end of your innings, a consistent bias can prompt an optional adjustment. Apply accepts it; Not now keeps your setting. The game never changes it automatically.
Weather
Weather changes as match time passes. The corner arrow shows wind direction and rounded speed. Open Settings for the full conditions. Air conditions influence swing; moisture lowers bounce and pace off the strip. A short pre-delivery notice explains a changed field or weather, then clears before the run-up.
Conditions
The pitch report shows its preparation and ball age. Grass helps seam; dry wear helps spin and makes bounce less even. Test pitches wear across innings and days. A new red ball is taken automatically after 80 overs. T20 has a six-over powerplay, then up to five boundary fielders; the death overs bring more yorkers and changes of pace.
Overs
Press Start over once. The deliveries follow automatically, with time to see each result. Wides and no-balls are rebowled. Play pauses between overs and at an innings break. The pause button always stops play.
Fast-forward
During the chase and return, tap Fast-forward 4× to speed up playback. Tap Normal speed to slow it again. Calling or cancelling a run restores normal speed. The ball, fielders and runners use the same clock. The next delivery always plays at normal speed.
Running
The first safe single from an attacking stroke starts automatically. Defence holds the crease; you can still call a safe run. Tap Run 2 or Run 3 to call another, using the available/tight/too-late cue. Three completed runs is the limit per delivery; there is no Run 4 option. Only one further run can be queued at a time. Cancel queued run cancels a run before it starts. A run in progress must be completed. Watch the throw: you can be run out if the bails are removed before you ground your bat. Completed runs still count when the next run fails.
Dismissals & extras
LBW checks where the ball pitched, where it hit the pad, whether you offered a shot and whether it would hit the stumps. Byes and leg byes go to extras. Leg byes require a shot or evasive action. A front-foot no-ball adds one and is rebowled; in T20 the next legal delivery is a free hit. You can still be run out from a no-ball or free hit.
Read movement
Swing curves in flight. Seam deviates at the bounce. Leg-spin turns away from a right-hander; a googly turns in.
Find a gap
The captain adapts the field to repeated boundaries and singles by the batter on strike. Read the field note before each delivery; the field map and pitch view show the same positions. After contact, a steady fielding view shows the chase, catch and return. Watch the hands to see a catch, or the bounce before a pickup. The batsman prepares your named shot as you drag. Swiping before a ball lets you practise without using a delivery.

T20 is 20 overs per side. Tests have two innings per side and five days. Your own batting is always ball by ball. When your team bowls, play every over with swipe-release bowling, or use Simulate over or Simulate innings. Select League for fixtures, tables and promotion. Open the menu between balls to declare a Test innings. A first-innings lead of 200 lets you enforce the follow-on. Each bowler keeps one bowling style for the whole over, with variations within that style. T20 introduces spin in the second over. Tests open with pace spells before spin enters. Each T20 bowler can deliver four overs. You have ten wickets. Bowled, caught, LBW and run-out dismissals are supported. Wides and no-balls do not count in the over. Byes and leg byes do not count against the bowler.

Perfect timing describes your release. Swipe length sets stroke strength. The ball loses speed through the air, on each bounce and while rolling on grass. Shot choice and the field still matter. A middled hit has a fuller bat sound; a nick has a sharp click. Sound can be switched off in the menu. The animation gives you more time than a real delivery. Listed speeds describe each bowler's pace. The XIs use real player names, with original game ratings and fantasy team selections. They are editable in the scorecard. Batting stance and the shot guide support both hands. Batting hand can be edited in the scorecard between deliveries.
